# Setting up: the commands, and the standing each needs

What an administrator or an owner does on the pages, their agent does here with the same standing, named beside them on the feed ([ADR-0021](/docs/adr/0021-a-token-configures-as-its-person-does.md)). What the person may not, the console refuses (`auth.forbidden`): that step goes back to them in the account, with who may do it, never worked around. `console help <command>` says every flag, limit and refusal; this page says which command does what, and who may.

## The project, for an administrator to make and an owner to keep

- **Made:** `console project new <key> "<name>" [--description <markdown>] [--visibility workspace|private] [--skills <address>] [--color <name>] [--symbol <text>]`, by an administrator alone ([ADR-0045](/docs/adr/0045-a-project-is-made-by-an-administrator.md)), who is then its owner. Private unless `--visibility workspace` says everyone of the workspace works in it. The key is lowercase letters, digits and hyphens and does not change once made: chosen with the person, and `project.key_taken` is not guessed around. The organization's skills are the project's unless `--skills` names an address of its own.
- **Changed:** `console project edit <key> [--name "<name>"] [the same flags; none clears]`, for an owner; its mark, `console project icon <key> <file>` (a PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP) or `--remove`.
- **Read:** `console project <key>` in full; `console projects` lists those the person may work in, with what they are in each.

## A plugin's board project, for an administrator

A plugin the deployment installed ([building-a-plugin](/skills/building-a-plugin/SKILL.md) writes one; the deployment installs it) does nothing until an administrator enables it, consenting to what it requires and turning on what they choose of what it can go without (`console plugin <key> capabilities` says which, and turns them on or off later), and one that puts decisions, tasks and pages on a board names its project then: `console plugin <key> enable --consent <capability>,... --board new|<key> [--projects all|<key>,...] [--set <setting>=<value>]...`, `new` making a private project from the plugin's own defaults (its key, name and description, which `console plugin <key>` shows beforehand, with what each capability opens), `<key>` binding a project that exists; several plugins may share one. `--consent` must name every capability the manifest asks, each read as the sentence it is: what the plugin may do and reach. The project's members are listed as any project's; the plugin's own roles are granted apart, `console plugin <key> grant <role> --person <login>|--project <key>|--everyone`, and taken back with `console plugin <key> revoke <grant>`; `console plugin <key> settings --set <setting>=<value>` fills its settings, and a secret is set by a person: typed unseen at the prompt of `console plugin <key> secret <name>` in their own terminal, or on the plugin's settings page; never an argument, which a shell keeps, and never through an agent, whose transcript would keep it (a script redirects a file into the command). From then on the rules an agent reads in that project end with `## Plugins`, naming it ([working-the-board](/skills/working-the-board/SKILL.md)), and what the plugin writes there carries its name beside the person's. `console plugin <key> disable` stops it and keeps its data. An owner who is not an administrator enables no plugin: the step goes back to the administrator, with the command.

## The members, for an owner

`console member add <login> [--role owner|member] [--project <key>[,<key>...]]`, `console member role <login> <owner|member>`, `console member remove <login>`; `console members` lists them. A login is matched as typed, so a person the list hides (where who sees whom is narrowed) is still listed by their login. In a project open to the workspace everyone is a member already: `member add <login> --role owner` lists an owner, `member role <login> member` takes the listing back, and a plain `member add` is refused (`member.open_project`).

## The categories, for an administrator

`console category new <key> "<name>" [--color <name>]`, `console category edit <key> [--name "<name>"] [--color <name>]`, `console category remove <key>`; `console categories` lists them, the same in every project. A category tasks or goals are sorted into stays until they are moved (`category.in_use`: `console task edit <number> --category <other|none>` moves one). Colours: gray, blue, green, orange, pink, purple, teal, yellow.

## The people, for an administrator

- `console person role <login> <admin|member>`: what a person is in the workspace; the board keeps at least one administrator in (`person.last_admin`).
- `console person manager <login> <manager login|none>`: whom a person reports to, the reporting line of the directory, which `console people` shows beside each person and the plugins read as a default (the approver of their leave, say) in place of a line of their own; another active person who does not report to them, directly or through others (`person.invalid_manager`), or `none`.
- `console person add <login> [--host gh|google]` lets a login in: whoever signs in with that account at that host is a person from then on; the host is read off the login (an address is Google's, anything else GitHub's) unless `--host` says. `console person remove <login>` takes a login away that nobody has signed in with yet.
- `console person deactivate <login>` takes a person out: refused on every request at once, their agents with them, kept with everything they did, and listed nowhere ([ADR-0044](/docs/adr/0044-a-token-of-a-person-who-is-out-is-kept-and-refused-with-the-reason-until-they-are-back-in.md)); `console person reactivate <login>` takes them back in. A person the organization let in, through its domain or its organization at the git host, comes and goes with it (`person.of_organization`).
- `console person title <login> "<title>"|none`: what a member does, in a line (`Release engineer`), shown under their name and printed by `console people`; every member's, a person's as an agent account's, and of an agent account its supervisors write it too.
- `console people` lists the people the person sees, by login, with what each is, and the logins let in that nobody has signed in with yet.

## An agent account, for an administrator

An agent account is an agent that is a member of its own ([ADR-0084](/docs/adr/0084-an-agent-account-is-a-member-of-its-own-with-a-manager-above-it.md)): it signs in through the identity provider with an account of its own, is assigned tasks, is asked and answers, and reports to a manager, under the rules a person is under and never as an administrator. It is not the person's own agent, which acts as them with a token: ask which is meant when "add an agent" could be either.

- `console agent new <handle> [--login <login>] [--name "<name>"] [--title "<title>"] [--manager <login|me>] [--project <key>]` makes one: the handle it is called by (`@mira`), which never changes; the account it will sign in with, a login at the git host or an address at Google, made of the workspace's short name, the handle and its domain where the workspace names its identity service and `--login` is left out; its manager, the person asking when none is said; and the project it starts in, the directory's unless `--project` says. The member's row is written at once, so that tasks are assigned to it before anyone signed in with it (`--worker @mira`). The pages make one from proposals instead: Agents, "Add an agent account".
- The account it signs in with has to be there at the identity provider before its machine is set up, and at a git host before the agent account is added, since a login there goes to whoever registers it and the console lets in whoever holds it until the account's first sign-in: say its exact login to the person, and ask who makes it where it is not there, a person or an agent that keeps the organization's accounts. Nothing here makes that account. What it signs in with is asked for once, by the agent on its machine, of whoever is there; no password is ever written in a task, a comment or a command.
- `console agents` lists the agent accounts, `console agent <handle>` reads one. Its role, its manager and whether it is in are a member's, said with `console person`, by its login or its handle: `console person manager @mira <login>`, `console person deactivate @mira`. An agent account is never made an administrator (`person.agent_admin`), and its manager is replaced, never taken away (`person.manager_required`).
- Its secrets are its own ([ADR-0087](/docs/adr/0087-an-agent-account-keeps-its-secrets-in-a-store-of-its-own-that-only-it-reads.md)): a supervisor, an administrator or whoever is above it in the reporting line, hands it one with `console agent secret <handle> <name>`, typed unseen by the person at their own terminal, never through the agent's chat and never as an argument; only the account reads it back.
- What a supervisor tells it reaches its runner as a signal ([ADR-0085](/docs/adr/0085-a-runner-on-the-agents-machine-is-told-by-signals-and-runs-one-session-at-a-time.md)), in the supervisor's own name and on the account's record: `console agent tell <handle> "<words>"` says something to it, `console agent interrupt <handle> "<words>"` stops its turn first, `console agent stop <handle>` stops it until `console agent restart <handle>`, which goes on with the same conversation, or `console agent reset <handle>`, which begins a new one, and `console agent set <handle> --model <name> --effort <level>` chooses what it runs with, among what its product offers. `console agent <handle>` says how it stands, `running`, `stopped`, `refused`, `failed` or `unheard`, and `console agent signals <handle>` reads the record. A person watches it on the pages: Agents, then its name, shows what it is doing as it happens, its sessions, its plate and what is kept of it. Stopping, restarting and resetting an agent are its supervisor's to decide: send one when the person asked for exactly that.

## The workspace, for an administrator

`console workspace` says its settings; `console workspace edit` changes them ([ADR-0035](/docs/adr/0035-who-may-see-whom-is-decided-by-one-policy-and-the-workspace-keeps-its-settings.md)): `--people-visibility <everyone|shared-projects>`, who sees whom on the People page and in the pickers; `--week-start <auto|monday|sunday>`, the day the calendars begin a week on; `--time-zone <zone|none>`, an IANA name such as `Asia/Seoul`, in which the command prints moments from then on. What the organization's agents work with is named there too ([ADR-0089](/docs/adr/0089-a-workspace-names-the-services-its-agents-work-with.md)): `--handle <name|none>`, the organization's short name (`acme`); `--domain <domain|none>`, its mail and web domain; and `--service <role>=<kind|none>[,<parameter>=<value>]...` for each of `identity`, `git` and `messenger`, as `--service identity=google-workspace --service git=github,organization=acme --service messenger=slack,workspace=acme.slack.com`. No secret goes there. With them named, an agent account's login is put together for it: `console agent new mira` signs in as `mira@acme.example`.

## The rules, for an owner or an administrator

`console rules` reads what holds: the workspace's rules, the console's own until an administrator writes the organization's, and the project's own. `console rules write --file <path>` writes the project's, for an owner, from [the example](project-rules.md); with `--workspace`, the organization's, for an administrator, starting from the console's own as `console rules` prints them; `none` puts the written rules away, so that the console's own hold again. Written only when a person asked for exactly that, and never to loosen what they ask of agents: every agent reads them.

## The goals, for an owner

`console goal new "<title>" [--body <markdown>] [--state <state>] [--category <key>] [--starts <day>] [--due <day>]` sets a goal on the roadmap; `console goal edit <id> [the same flags; none clears a category or a day]` changes one; `console goals` lists them, `--search <words>` by the words of a title; `console task archive --goal <id>` puts every done or dropped task of a goal away at once.

## A private pack, for an owner or an administrator

A project whose skills are a private repository holds a SkillCDN token the console reads them with ([ADR-0028](/docs/adr/0028-the-console-reads-a-projects-pack-through-the-deployments-rest-api-with-a-pack-token-for-a-private-one.md)): `console project pack-token <key> --connect`, for an owner; for the organization's pack, which every project reads that names no address and holds no token of its own, `console workspace pack-token --connect`, for an administrator ([ADR-0036](/docs/adr/0036-a-private-pack-is-connected-with-a-code-a-person-approves-from-the-projects-settings.md)). The command prints a page at SkillCDN and a code, which the agent relays; the person approves there, signed in as someone who may open the repository, and the console keeps the token, shown to nobody. `--stdin` reads a token a person made by hand at SkillCDN from standard input, never an argument: one for the pack's repository, or, for a pack a deployment serves from a directory of its own and lets nobody read without a token, the one its operator made, which no code connects since nobody signs in there; `--remove` takes it away. Until a token is set, `console skills` says the pack is not served to the console.
