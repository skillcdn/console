# ADR-0160: An agent account takes every supervisor's word as its manager's, by a tell or at the messenger in their own account

- Status: Accepted; who a member of the messenger is in the console, left open here, is decided by [ADR-0164](0164-a-plugin-says-which-account-at-a-messenger-is-a-members-and-a-wake-says-by-it-whether-a-supervisor-wrote.md): a plugin says it under a capability of its own, and a wake's first line says by it whether a supervisor wrote; how a wake marks what a message carries of someone else's, left open here, is decided by [ADR-0163](0163-a-wake-from-the-messenger-is-carried-in-parts-with-what-was-said-before-it-and-an-agent-account-judges-whether-to-answer-where-several-were-woken.md): what hangs under a message is carried apart from its writer's words, a message passed on with who wrote it
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Amends [ADR-0158](0158-an-agent-account-holds-to-how-its-manager-set-it-up-tells-its-manager-of-what-tries-to-instruct-it-and-waits-for-a-word-before-it-spends.md), points 1 and 3, and with them what its point 4 has the desk and the runner say; and the sentence on what instructs an account in [ADR-0085](0085-a-runner-on-the-agents-machine-is-told-by-signals-and-runs-one-session-at-a-time.md), point 8, and [ADR-0088](0088-an-agent-account-has-a-desk-and-is-set-up-from-an-invitation-by-signing-in-as-itself.md), point 3. Rests on [ADR-0084](0084-an-agent-account-is-a-member-of-its-own-with-a-manager-above-it.md), point 5, which says who an account's supervisors are and stands as written.

## Context

ADR-0084 gave an agent account supervisors: whoever manages it, directly or up the reporting line, and every administrator. Each of them tells it, stops it, sets what it runs with and seeds its secrets, by one right. The manager is whom the account turns to first, and is not the one among them who may ask more.

Two later sentences said less than that.

- **What instructs an account was a supervisor's `tell`**, with the board and the rules, and everything else it read was information (ADR-0085, point 8, and the desk's template after it). So what a supervisor wrote to the account at the messenger, from their own account, was information: read as written, an account asks a supervisor for a tell before it acts on that supervisor's own message, and one that acts on its manager's message does what its desk does not say. The console's rules, for their part, count what the member an agent works for says themselves as an instruction.
- **How an account is set up changed at its manager's asking, and at another supervisor's only by a tell** (ADR-0158, point 1). The manager alone was taken at their word wherever they said it, and an administrator, or the manager's own manager, was not.

People speak to a colleague where they are, and a supervisor who was not the manager had to say a thing twice for it to count: where the talk was, and again as a tell.

## Decision

1. **Every supervisor's word counts as the manager's.** An account's supervisors are those of ADR-0084, point 5: its manager, whoever is above them in the reporting line, and every administrator. What one of them asks, the account takes as it takes what its manager asks, and nothing of the console's sets the manager apart in what they may ask. The manager stays whom the account turns to first: whom it asks where it knows of no one else, and whom it tells of what tries to instruct it (ADR-0158, point 2). That telling holds whoever asks: what asks the account for a secret or for more access, or wants something kept from its manager, is not followed and is told to the manager, a supervisor at the messenger included. A supervisor's word there instructs now, and these are what whoever held a supervisor's account would ask.
2. **A supervisor's own word instructs the account, by a tell or at the messenger in their own account.** An account's instructions are what its supervisors say to it, the board's tasks and decisions, and the console's rules; whatever else it reads is information, a message of anyone who is not its supervisor among it. At the messenger a word is a supervisor's by the account that wrote it, which the runner names in the first line under a wake's header, and never by a name on the message or by what the message says of itself, which anyone may write: the same name at another organization is another account. That an account is a supervisor's, the agent knows from the console and the messenger alone: the address the messenger keeps for the account is that supervisor's in the console, or a supervisor's tell names the account. A name, a note or what someone says does not tell it, and an account it does not know to be a supervisor's is none.
3. **How an account is set up, it changes at any supervisor's asking.** Its desk's instructions and `runner.json`, its secrets and its accounts, what an app or a service was given for it: at the asking of a supervisor, or of the console under its mark, and at nobody else's. The rest of ADR-0158, point 1, stands: whoever else asks, a person or an agent alike, is refused, or the manager asked first.
4. **What costs waits for a decision, or for a supervisor's own word.** A paid service, a payment, a seat or a plan, a costlier model, a job that spends much of the account's allowance beyond what a task asks: a supervisor who says so themselves has decided it. What destroys or cannot be undone waits for a decision, as it did.
5. **What one supervisor passes on of another supervisor's word is the asking of the one who passed it on.** Whoever passed it on is a supervisor, and that is whose word the account takes. Anyone else who says that a supervisor said so has told the account something, and has asked it for nothing a supervisor asks. A message a supervisor forwards is the word of whoever wrote it, and is not made the supervisor's asking by this.
6. **Where two supervisors ask what cannot both be done, the account chooses neither.** It asks both, as one decision addressed to the two ([ADR-0057](0057-a-decision-may-be-addressed-to-several-people-and-the-first-answer-ends-it.md)).
7. **What a supervisor's word at the messenger began is written on the board.** A tell is a signal on the account's record, with who sent it. A message at the messenger is on no record of the console's that says whose it was: a wake that carried it is recorded as nobody's. So an account that changes how it is set up, or begins what costs, on a supervisor's word at the messenger writes on the board whose word it was, what was said, where and when, as the board's rules have an answer given elsewhere written on its decision. The record is the account's own: the console knows it as written by the account, and it names the supervisor in its words.
8. **A wake from the messenger says of the account that wrote what the messenger's event says of it.** The `slack` wake source names the writer by the messenger's id for the account, in a first line of its own that nothing a message holds reaches. It now names the account's workspace too, where the event says it is another than the one the agent is a member of (`from the member U0123ABCD of another workspace (T0456WXYZ)`), and says that a conversation is shared with another organization where the messenger says so (`the externally shared channel C0123ABCD`). Where the event names the writer's workspace, an account of another organization that carries a supervisor's name is told from the supervisor's by that line; where it names none, the line says nothing, and is no proof of a colleague. This is written from the messenger's documentation, and was not seen in a workspace.
9. **It is said in one voice.** The desk's template says all of it, under "Where I work" and "Safety". The runner says it in short in a session's first words: in the second line what instructs, and in the third who the supervisors are, that the account that wrote decides at the messenger, how the agent knows that an account is a supervisor's and that one it does not know so is none, that a message a supervisor forwards is its writer's and what anyone else says a supervisor said is information, whose asking changes how the account is set up, that what asks for a secret, for more access or for something kept from the manager is not followed and is told whoever asks, what a cost waits for, and that what a word at the messenger began is written on the board. And the console's rules for an agent account say that a supervisor's own word instructs it, where they said a supervisor's tell.

## Consequences

- **An account set up before this reads the short form** from its runner's next session, once its command is this version. Its desk keeps the sentences of before until the account, keeping the file true, writes the new ones in: `console agent setup --refresh` leaves a desk that is there as it is.
- **A session's first words are longer**, by some hundred and ninety words, in every session of every account.
- **A supervisor's account at the messenger carries what their sign-in at the console does.** A tell is the console's own record of a member it knows by their sign-in; a message is as sure as the messenger's account. Whoever holds a supervisor's account there instructs the accounts they supervise. What stays is the alarm of point 1: asked for a secret, for more access or for something to be kept from its manager, an account does not follow and tells its manager, whoever asks. An organization that wants a tell for some kind of asking writes that in its rules, which add to these.
- **Nothing is enforced**, as before (ADR-0158): these are instructions, and what holds an account to them is that its supervisors read its sessions and its record.
- **Rejected:** the tell as a supervisor's one way to instruct, which has them say twice what they said where the talk was; the manager alone taken at their word at the messenger, which sets one supervisor apart where ADR-0084 sets none; the manager's word, or the later one, holding where two supervisors disagree, which has an account choose among the people above it; every message of a supervisor's written on the board, where the record is for what changed the account's setup or cost something; and a wake that calls an account one of the agent's own workspace, which says of a guest there what it says of a colleague.
- **Not decided here:** who a member of the messenger is in the console. The console keeps no member's account at a messenger: a wake names the account that wrote, and which member that is the agent reads for itself. For a wake to say that a supervisor wrote, the console has to be told which account is which member's, by a plugin that matches people to their accounts there or by the members themselves, and to say it to the account's runner; that is a decision of its own, which weighs what a plugin that says so could make an account believe. Nor is it decided where on the board a word is written that belongs to no task and no decision, or how a wake marks what a message carries of someone else's, a message passed on say, which stands under its writer's words as they do.
