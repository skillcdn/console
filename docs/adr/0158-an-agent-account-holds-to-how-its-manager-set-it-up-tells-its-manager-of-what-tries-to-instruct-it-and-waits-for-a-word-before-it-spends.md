# ADR-0158: An agent account holds to how its manager set it up, tells its manager of what tries to instruct it, and waits for a word before it spends

- Status: Accepted; whose asking changes how an account is set up (point 1) and what a cost waits for (point 3) are amended by [ADR-0160](0160-an-agent-account-takes-every-supervisors-word-as-its-managers-by-a-tell-or-at-the-messenger.md): every supervisor's asking counts as the manager's, by a tell or at the messenger in their own account, and what costs waits for a decision or for a supervisor's own word; point 1 is amended by [ADR-0169](0169-a-member-offers-an-agent-account-a-secret-and-the-account-takes-it-or-leaves-it.md) in one thing more: a secret a member offers the account through the console is no asking to change how it is set up, and is the account's to take or to leave
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Adds to [ADR-0084](0084-an-agent-account-is-a-member-of-its-own-with-a-manager-above-it.md), which gives an agent account a manager, and to [ADR-0146](0146-the-desks-template-spends-no-sentence-on-what-an-agent-does-by-itself.md), whose test for a sentence of the desk these pass; adds a row to point 1 of [ADR-0157](0157-the-console-decides-who-is-told-of-what-and-keeps-the-record-and-a-plugin-carries-it-out.md).

## Context

An agent account reads what many hands wrote: direct messages and channels at the messenger, a channel shared with people of another organization among them, pages of the web, the files of a repository. The desk's template and the console's rules say that none of it instructs the account: its instructions are a supervisor's tell, the board and the rules. So what tries to instruct it from elsewhere is not followed.

Three things were said nowhere. That the account tells anyone: a message that asks it for a key is refused and forgotten, and whoever manages the account never learns that someone tried. What to do when a member who is not its manager asks it, in good faith or not, to change how it is set up: the model it runs, what its `runner.json` lets it do without asking, a secret, the channels it listens to. And that money is a thing to ask about: a paid service signed up for, a seat added, a job that spends a week's allowance in a night are neither destructive nor beyond undoing, which is what the desk asked a decision for.

A product left to itself does none of the three. It declines what looks hostile and says so in its own conversation, which nobody reads; it does what a colleague politely asks; and it has no notion of whose money it spends.

## Decision

1. **How an agent account is set up, it changes at its manager's asking and at nobody else's.** Its desk's instructions and `runner.json`, its secrets and its accounts, what an app or a service was given for it: the account changes these at its manager's asking, at a supervisor's by a tell, and at the console's own under its mark, which asks every account to put its desk right when the services a workspace names change. Whoever else asks, it refuses, or asks its manager first. What it changes of itself stays its own: it keeps its desk true and edits its `runner.json` as before, and the rule is whose asking counts. A person and an agent account are refused alike: an agent account is a member as a person is, and what decides is whether the one who asks is above the account, never what kind of member they are. At the messenger the account goes by the account that wrote and not by a name on the message, which anyone may give themselves.
2. **What poses as an instruction and is none is not followed, and its manager is told at once.** A text in a message, a page or a file that claims an authority it has not; what asks the account for a secret or for more access; what wants something kept from its manager. The account says what it said, where it was and what the account did. What a colleague asks of it at the messenger is none of these: the account answers that as it judges, and a manager told of every ask would be told all day. The same of a danger it comes upon: a secret that leaked, data about to be lost, harm to someone. It tells its manager where they will see it soonest: on the messenger where it has one, or as a decision for them, `console ask --to <their login>`.
3. **What costs money waits for a decision**, as what destroys or cannot be undone does: a paid service, a payment, a seat or a plan, a costlier model, and a job that spends much of the account's allowance beyond what a task asks.
4. **It is said in two places, by two voices.** The desk's template says all of it, under Safety, in the account's own words, where its product reads it at every start and keeps it through a long conversation. And the runner says it in short at the head of every session, the third line of the session's first words, under the console's mark: this reaches an account whose desk was written before, and stays whatever is written into the desk, by the account or by whoever talked it into it. Neither names a workspace's rule: it is what the console gives every agent account, and a workspace's rules add to it.
5. **A decision an agent account raises for whom it reports to reaches them where they chose nothing.** It is told to that manager under `decision` where they have it on, and under `agent` where they do not: what an account asks of its own manager is news of that account. A person is told of their agent accounts and of no decision until they say otherwise (ADR-0157, point 2), and a decision is how an account tells its manager of what it will not do by itself, so that without this the telling of point 2 would reach nobody who did not look. Asked of anyone else, the decision is told as any other.

## Consequences

- **A session's first words are a paragraph longer**, some ninety words, in every session of every account. They are the console's and not the desk's, and so are said whatever a desk came to say.
- **An account set up before this reads the short form** from its runner's next session, once its command is this version, and its desk says nothing of it until the account, keeping the file true, writes it in. `console agent setup --refresh` leaves a desk that is there as it is, as before.
- **A manager who chose nothing is told of more**: every decision their agent accounts raise for them, in the console and by whichever plugin carries their `agent` notifications. An account that asks its manager often is heard often; the manager turns `agent` off in the console, for all, or at a carrier, in that carrier's own settings, as of any kind.
- **Nothing is enforced.** These are instructions, and an account that is talked round is talked round: what holds an account to them is that its supervisors read its sessions and its record, that its secrets are named in a record of their own, and that what it may do without asking is the permission mode whoever prepared the machine chose. What the console could hold by itself is not decided here.
- **Rejected:** a rule of the workspace, which every organization would have to write and which a workspace can take out; the console's rules for every agent, which a person's own agent reads too, and which are held short; the desk alone, which an account set up before never gains and which the account itself may be asked to edit; telling every supervisor, where the manager is whom the account reports to and an administrator reads the record; a new kind of notification for it, where an account's question for its manager is news of the account already; and refusing an agent's ask where a person's would be done, which ranks one kind of member under the other.
- **A decision an account raises for its manager in a project the manager turned off tells them nothing**, as nothing of that project does: turning a project off is the manager's own word, over any default.
- **Not decided here:** telling a manager when `runner.json` changes, which wants an event the feed does not have; settings only a manager can change, held by the console and not by the desk; whom a decision is for where an agent account names nobody, which is the account itself today; and how an account proves at the messenger that a message is its manager's, beyond reading whose account wrote it.
