# ADR-0019: A task is planned in days, the board keeps its moments, and goals and categories give the work its shape

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-11

## Context

An organization running its work on the board needs more than a column per state. Whoever runs a project has to see whether the work is on its days, who is late and what is not scheduled at all; a worker needs their own work in front of them across the projects they are in, without treading on what another person's agent is already at; an agent needs the same facts in as few words as possible, so that its context goes to the work. The board gave each task a state and a priority and nothing of time, nothing of kind, and nothing of why. The decisions, read as one long feed of cards, were right for the few that wait and wrong for the many that were answered.

## Decision

1. **A task's plan is two days, and what happened to it is two moments the board keeps itself.** `startsOn` and `dueOn` are calendar days (`2026-10-24`), set by people and their agents, the same day wherever the people are; the due day is not before the start. `startedAt` and `doneAt` are instants the board writes: the first move past `ready` is the start, the move to `done` is the end, and a move back out of `done` takes the end away. Nobody types a completion date, since the board already knows the moment, and nobody confuses a plan with a fact.
2. **Categories are the workspace's vocabulary for the kinds of work.** A category has a key (what the command and the filters say; immutable), a name and a colour from a fixed list of names, so that nothing typed reaches a stylesheet. Every workspace starts with the usual kinds (engineering, product, design, marketing, operations); an administrator renames, recolours, adds and removes them, and a category tasks are sorted into cannot be removed until they are moved. A task carries one or none. Kept at the workspace and not per project, so that a kind of work means the same on every board and a person's own work reads the same across projects.
3. **A roadmap is a project's goals, and a goal's progress is read from its tasks.** A goal has a title, a body in Markdown, a state (`planned`, `active`, `done`, `dropped`), and the day it is meant to be reached by. A task works towards a goal through `goalId`. Progress is never written down: it is the goal's tasks counted by state, as current as the board. An owner sets and changes goals, from the console's own pages or through an agent of theirs; a person says when a goal is reached, since a goal is an outcome and not a sum.
4. **The views follow the people.** The board is narrowed by category, goal, person and one's own, each a change of its address. A project's calendar shows the month with each task on its due day, the late and the unscheduled below it, and who has what: the open tasks of each person with what of them is late or due within the week. A project's roadmap shows the goals with their progress. The workspace has a page of what is on the person's plate across the projects they may see, their open tasks by when they are due and the decisions waiting for a person. The decisions page keeps the waiting ones as cards, since they are what a person came for, and turns the answered ones into a record, a row each, since they are looked up and not read through. A card on the board says the number and the title, the category, what is due, who is on it, what blocks it, and whose agent is at it, and nothing else.
5. **The surfaces follow.** The REST API takes and answers the new fields on a task, narrows the board by them, serves the categories at `/api/v1/categories`, a project's goals at `/api/v1/projects/<key>/goals`, and a person's plate at `/api/v1/work`; the command gets `work`, `goals`, `goal`, `categories`, the filters of `tasks`, the flags of `task new` and a `task edit`; the default UI gets the filters, the Calendar and Roadmap pages of a project, and the My work and Categories pages of the workspace.

## Consequences

- A manager reads whether the work is on its days from the calendar and from who has what; a worker reads their own work from one page; an agent reads the plan from one line of `console task` and narrows the board with one flag. None of it is typed twice.
- The moments on a task are only as true as the moves: a task moved to done by mistake and back has its end taken away, which is right; a task done before the board kept the moment has the time it was last changed, as near as the rows could say, which the migration wrote once.
- A run an agent never ended keeps a task "at work" until a person marks the run abandoned; whether a run with no report for a long while should be ended by the board itself is a decision of its own, left open.
- Categories are bounded per workspace and removed only when unused; a kind of work an organization is done with is renamed, not deleted from history.
- Rejected: completion dates typed by hand (always late or forgotten, and the board knows the moment); a planned start kept in step with the actual one (the plan and what happened are two different facts, and a manager needs both); categories per project (the same kind of work would mean different things on different boards); goals with a stored percentage (it drifts from the board the moment it is written); goals across projects (the project is the unit of work and of permission, and a roadmap across them is a question for later); labels or tags on tasks (a vocabulary an administrator keeps stays small and means something; tags grow until they mean nothing); a notion of "online" for people (presence is noise on a board of work; the agents' last activity is kept on their tokens already).
