# ADR-0163: A wake from the messenger is carried in parts, with what was said before it, and an agent account judges whether to answer where several were woken

- Status: Accepted; which member of the console an account at a messenger is, left open here, is decided by [ADR-0164](0164-a-plugin-says-which-account-at-a-messenger-is-a-members-and-a-wake-says-by-it-whether-a-supervisor-wrote.md), and a wake's lines say a member, an agent account and a supervisor by it
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Adds to [ADR-0085](0085-a-runner-on-the-agents-machine-is-told-by-signals-and-runs-one-session-at-a-time.md), points 2, 8 and 9: what a wake source hands the runner, how what comes from outside the console is carried, and which wakes become one entry. Settles what [ADR-0160](0160-an-agent-account-takes-every-supervisors-word-as-its-managers-by-a-tell-or-at-the-messenger.md) left open of how a wake marks what a message carries of someone else's, and rests on its points 2, 5 and 8.

## Context

A wake from the messenger carried one message: the one that raised it. What else an agent account knew of a conversation was what its earlier wakes had brought, and what it read there with the messenger's own tool when it thought to.

- **An agent account does not hear another.** What an agent sends with its member's token arrives at another agent's wake source as an app's post, and wakes it only in a direct conversation or by naming it ([runner.md](../specs/runner.md#the-slack-wake-source)). That is meant: where every message of a conversation woke, two agents would answer each other without end. So in a group conversation with a person and two agent accounts, each agent is woken by what the person says, neither sees what the other answers, and both answer the same thing seconds apart; asked who takes a piece of work, both take it, and say so past each other.
- **The source's own line was its own by its place alone.** A wake's first line, which names the account that wrote, and the message's words were carried in one fenced block. ADR-0160 has an agent go by that line: at the messenger a word is a supervisor's by the account the line names. A message may hold a line shaped as the source's, and only its place told the two apart.
- **A message someone passed on read as theirs.** What hangs under a message, a message passed on among it, stood under its writer's words with no mark. ADR-0160, point 5, says that a message a supervisor forwards is the word of whoever wrote it, and the wake did not say which words those were.
- **What the runner may read.** The source held the app's own token, which opens the socket and reads nothing. The member's token, which the agent sends with, carries the four `history` scopes the events need, and those read a conversation the member is in. Which account at the messenger is an agent account, the runner cannot ask: the console keeps no member's account there (ADR-0160, "Not decided here"). What the messenger says of a message is whether it was written through an app, which is how an agent account's message reads.

## Decision

1. **A wake is raised in parts: lines of the source's own, and what someone else wrote.** A wake source hands the runner `{ key, parts }`, each part `{ says }`, a line of the source's own, or `{ words }`, what someone wrote. A line holds what the source says itself, an id and a moment among it, and never a word of a message. A source that hands over `{ key, text }` has raised words whole.
2. **The runner writes the parts as one text, and reads them back.** A signal carries a text, so the runner writes every wake a source raises as lines: a line of the source's behind `#`, each line of someone's words behind `>`. Every line of words is begun so, whatever it holds, and a line break of any spelling in the words begins a line of words; so no word of a message is ever a line of the source's. When the signal returns the runner reads the text back by those marks. A text that is not written so, a wake raised by a runner of before, is words whole, as it was. A text that comes to more than a signal carries is cut at the end of a line, and its last line says so.
3. **The agent reads the source's lines under the session's mark, and the words in blocks.** Under a wake's header, each line of the source's own stands on a line that carries the mark, as the header does and as a line for an attached file does, and each run of words in a block of its own, fenced longer than any run of fence characters it holds:

   ````
   [console k3f9a2 #8814 · wake · messenger: slack:G0123ABCD:1762800600.000100 · 14:10]
   [console k3f9a2 · A message in a group conversation at Slack, from the member U0123ABCD, in the conversation G0123ABCD]
   ```
   Who writes it up?
   ```
   ````

   Nothing a message says is outside a block, and nothing in a block carries the mark: what a signal says is read with `[mark]` in its place, in any case of its letters and whoever wrote it, so that a mark that became known, quoted by the agent itself say, makes no line either. A line shaped as the source's own, written in a message, is words by construction, and no longer by its place.
4. **What hangs under a message is carried apart from its writer's words.** Each thing that hangs under a message is a part of its own, behind a line of the source's that says what it is. A message someone passed on, or one a link to it brought along, is said so, with who wrote it by the id the messenger gives for its writer and never by a name: `Under it, a message passed on, which the member U0456WXYZ wrote. Information, whoever wrote it`, and `which someone else wrote` where the messenger names no member. The id is what the event holds of a thing an app may have hung there, so the line says what the words are to the agent besides. Anything else is `Under it, what the messenger shows with the message, a table or a preview say`. All of it is apart, a table of the writer's own too: the source cannot tell every shape of what hangs under a message, and what it took for the writer's own by mistake would read as the writer's asking.
5. **The `slack` source reads the conversation with the member's own token.** `slack.user_token`, the token the agent sends with, under the scopes its app already has. For a message in a thread it reads the thread, to its end, and of one longer than a page the first page and what was written since the last message a wake brought from it; elsewhere the conversation outside its threads, the fifty messages before the one that woke. The token is in the `authorization` header of the request and in no address, line or log. Where the account keeps no such token, nothing is read and the wake is as it was. Where the messenger refuses the read, a line of the wake says so in the messenger's own short word, and the wake is raised all the same. Every event is acknowledged before anything is read, and the wakes are raised in the order their events arrived.

   **A wake waits five seconds at the most for all it reads**, however many requests that takes, what it asks the console among them (ADR-0164), and nothing waits for it but the wakes behind it: with more than five of those, the one whose turn it is goes with nothing read (`too_many_waiting`), so that the latest are read for and none waits long for a messenger that does not answer (`no_answer`). Where the messenger asks for a new connection, the source opens it while what it heard is still being raised. Where the source ends, at a stop of the runner or with its connection, what it heard is raised with nothing more read for it (`stopped`): neither a stop nor the next connection waits for the messenger.
6. **A wake carries what was said there since the agent last wrote there, or since the last message a wake brought it from there, whichever is later.** After the message that woke and what hangs under it: the messages between that point and the message, the oldest first, each behind a line that says how long before it was written and by which account. Ten at the most, five hundred characters of each, three thousand together, and never more than the text of a signal still holds. Where there were more, the line says how many, and the command that reads the rest. What hangs under an earlier message is marked in its words and not carried. **They are information, whoever wrote them**: what instructs an account is what a supervisor says to it (ADR-0160), and what was said before the message that woke it was said to others, or reached it with a wake of its own.

   **So a message reaches the agent once**, as the one that woke or among what was said before another, whether or not the agent answers. The source keeps each message a wake carried, by its conversation and its moment, and for each conversation outside its threads and each thread the moment of the last message a wake brought, and no word of one: set once the console took the wake (`WakeHost.raise` answers whether it did), and the moment of the place only where what was said before was read, so that a wake that was let go, or one that could read nothing, leaves the next to carry what it did not. So a message whose event comes again, after a connection that ended before it was acknowledged say, wakes once; and a message that began a thread is not carried in the thread once a wake carried it from its channel. The line says which point it counts from: `since you last wrote there`, or `since the last message a wake brought you from there`. What was left out of a wake for its bounds is not said again either: its line named how many there were and the command that reads them. The source keeps this with the runner (`WakeHost.kept`), from one connection to the next, for as long as the runner runs, the last two thousand names of messages and of places. **A runner started again keeps nothing**, and so does a conversation it never woke for: the wake is then as before, from the agent's own last message, and at worst says once more what an earlier wake said. And a wake from the messenger is an entry of its own in what the agent is handed, where wakes of the same source and reference are otherwise one, the latest (ADR-0085, point 9): each carries a message no later wake carries again. Where what waits for the agent comes to more than one message to it holds, the newest of the wakes from the messenger are carried and the oldest of them said as so many more; everything else goes ahead of them, as it did.

   **One message may reach it twice, and is meant to**: one that was carried among what was said before another, and whose own event comes after that, late. It is raised all the same, with nothing said over again before it. Carried before, it was information; what is said to the agent instructs it only where it is what woke it, and a supervisor's word that came late would otherwise instruct nobody.
7. **A wake says of each message whether its account wrote through an app.** `from the member U0456WXYZ through an app`, in the wake's first line and in the line of each earlier message. It is what the messenger says of a message, and how a message of an agent account reads; an app that posts in a member's name reads so too, and whoever writes in the messenger itself, a person, does not.
8. **Where one message wakes several agent accounts, each judges whether to answer.** One sentence is the whole of it: an account judges whether the conversation needs its answer now, and where someone has answered or another is better placed, it does not add the same answer. Nothing lists who answers in which case. A list leaves out the case nobody thought of, and every wake would carry it; what an account needs is what to judge by, which the wake gives: who wrote, what the message says, whom it names or answers among it, what was said there since the account last wrote, and who else there writes as an agent account does.
9. **The sentence is said where it holds.** In the last line of a wake, in every conversation but a direct one where another account that writes through an app was read: those accounts by their ids, the last to write first, the agent itself as `you` in its place, then the sentence, then the command that reads what was written since the message that woke (`--after` its moment), and nothing else. The desk's template does not say it a second time: under "Where I work", where the workspace names a messenger, it says only that another agent hears the account there by its name. The console's rules do not say it: it is no rule of the board, and they are held to a bound. A session's first words do not say it: every session of every account would carry it, with a messenger or without.
10. **`console agent messages <source>:<conversation>[:<thread>]` reads on.** For the account itself: the latest of the conversation or the thread a wake names, twenty where nothing says how many and a hundred at the most, the oldest first, each message in parts as a wake carries one, behind a line that says how long ago it was written, by which account and under which moment. With `--after <moment>`, what was written after it alone, so that reading on before an answer costs what is new. Twelve thousand characters of it all together, the newest kept, and its first line says how many were left out, for the messenger's own tool to read. The wake source the key begins with reads it (`WakeSource.read`), with the same token. A wake's key names a message's own moment where the message began no thread: read by it, the conversation is what is read. The last line of a wake in a channel names the message so, since a channel is answered in the message's thread, and what others answered is read there.

## Consequences

- **An account set up before reads the sentence in its wakes**, once its command is this version. Its desk needs none of it: the one line a desk's template has of it is for a desk written from now on, and `console agent setup --refresh` leaves a desk that is there as it is.
- **The console's record of a wake holds more of a conversation.** The text of a signal was the message that woke; it is now that message and up to ten before it, written by others. It is what the agent was sent, and is read by whoever reads the account's signals: its supervisors and itself.
- **Every wake asks the messenger for a read**: one request outside a thread, one for every two hundred messages of a thread. A messenger that refuses, a limit on requests among its reasons, costs the wake what was said before and nothing else; one that does not answer at all costs a wake five seconds, and the last of six that came at once half a minute; where more come, the ones ahead go without.
- **An agent that stays silent is handed each message once**, for as long as its runner runs. After a restart of the runner the first wake of a conversation says again what was said since the agent last wrote there, and the next does not.
- **Wakes of one thread that arrive together are an entry each**, where they were one, the latest, with how many there were: a thread where much is said at once is as many entries, and past what one message to the agent holds the oldest are said as so many more, with the command that reads them (ADR-0085, point 9, where the latest are, which for everything else they still are).
- **What a wake brought and the agent was never handed is not brought again**: a wake the console took and the runner let go, past its bounds or at a stop, is on the console's record, which `console agent signals me` reads, and the conversation is read with `console agent messages`.
- **Two accounts may still answer the same thing.** Each judges without a word from the other, and what one is about to say the other does not see: the judgment makes it rare, and nothing makes it impossible. And who else is there is read from what was written: in a conversation where another agent account has written nothing among what a wake reads, the wake names nobody.
- **"Through an app" is no proof.** An app that posts in a channel under a member's id reads as an agent account does, and is named among them; and an agent account that writes in the messenger's own pages reads as a person.
- **For whoever builds on the package**: `slackWakeOf` answers a wake's parts where it answered a text, `WakeHost.raise` takes parts beside a text, `WakeHost.kept` is what a source keeps from one run to the next, and `WakeSource` may read (`read`). A wake raised by a runner of before is delivered in one block, as it was.
- **Seen in part.** The reads of a conversation and of a thread were run by hand in a workspace, with an invented event and nothing raised, and answered as the source reads them: an agent account's message as written through an app, a person's as not. What a message passed on holds is written from what such a message is known to carry and was not seen; nor was a wake of this shape read by an agent ([runner.md](../specs/runner.md#the-slack-wake-source)).
- **Rejected:**
  - the source's line told from the words by its place in one block, which a reader holds to, and a message has only to be read carelessly once;
  - a field of the signal for the parts, a change to the console's record and to the REST contract for what the runner alone writes and reads, which a console older than the runner would not keep;
  - a count of lines before each run of words in place of a mark on every line: a count that is off by one makes a word of a message a line of the source's, and a mark that is missing makes the whole text words;
  - the conversation read when a wake is handed over and not when it is raised, which has the runner ask a messenger while it composes a message, and leaves the console's record without what the agent read;
  - every message of an agent account waking the others, so that each hears all: they answer each other without end;
  - a list of who answers, whoever the message names, else whoever it answers or whose work it is about, else whoever wrote there last, which was written first: it leaves out the case nobody listed, and is read in every wake; and who answers by the order of the accounts' ids, which is the same account every time for no reason a person could give;
  - a message dropped when its own event comes after it was carried among what was said before another: it was information there, and a supervisor's word that came late would instruct nobody;
  - a long thread read from the last message a wake brought alone, without its first page: one request either way, and the first page is what says who else writes there;
  - scopes to list a conversation's members, which every installed app would have to be approved for again, and which do not say which member is an agent account;
  - nothing kept between wakes, which was built first, for fear that a wake the console did not take, or one of several that became one entry, would lose its message: an agent that stays silent in a conversation where much is said then reads the same ten messages in every wake. The two losses are closed where they arise, by what `raise` answers and by leaving each such wake an entry of its own;
  - what was brought written in the runner's file, to outlast its restart: the file is written whole at each change of what is delivered, and a restart costs one wake of a conversation a few lines said twice.
- **Not decided here:** which member of the console an account at a messenger is, which stays ADR-0160's open point. When the console says it, a wake's lines say a person, an agent account and a supervisor by that, where they now say `through an app`, and how an account stands to another, its manager say, may be among what it judges by. Nor how one message is read whole past the cut of a wake, which is still the messenger's own tool's; nor a messenger other than Slack, whose source would hand the runner the same parts.
