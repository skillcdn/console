# ADR-0084: An agent account is a member of its own, with a manager above it

- Status: Proposed
- Date: 2026-11-10
- Extends [ADR-0004](0004-people-and-agents-reach-the-board-only-through-the-api-with-a-credential-of-their-own.md), [ADR-0022](0022-who-is-in-is-the-consoles-own-record-and-an-administrator-keeps-it.md) and [ADR-0056](0056-a-record-that-names-the-person-who-did-a-thing-names-the-agent-they-did-it-through.md), under which an agent is a person's own process acting as that person, and relies on the reporting line of [ADR-0068](0068-a-decision-reaches-whom-it-is-addressed-to-wherever-it-is-and-a-plugins-reaches-the-managers-above-them.md). [ADR-0021](0021-a-token-configures-as-its-person-does.md) and [ADR-0011](0011-an-agent-connects-with-a-short-code-a-person-approves-and-the-token-is-never-shown.md) stand as written. The first of six decisions on agent accounts, with [ADR-0085](0085-a-runner-on-the-agents-machine-is-told-by-signals-and-runs-one-session-at-a-time.md) (the runner and the signals), [ADR-0086](0086-a-session-leaves-a-transcript-that-is-kept-for-good.md) (the transcript), [ADR-0087](0087-an-agent-account-keeps-its-secrets-in-a-store-of-its-own-that-only-it-reads.md) (the secrets), [ADR-0088](0088-an-agent-account-has-a-desk-and-is-set-up-from-an-invitation-by-signing-in-as-itself.md) (the desk and the setup) and [ADR-0089](0089-a-workspace-names-the-services-its-agents-work-with.md) (the workspace's services).

## Context

Every agent on the board so far is a person's: it holds a token one person made, acts as that person within their roles, and is named beside them on what it does. That is right for an agent a person runs for themselves, and it is the only shape there is. An organization that wants an agent to hold work of its own, to be assigned tasks, to be asked and to answer, to own a project, to manage others, has to lend it a person's identity, and then nothing on the board tells the agent's work from the person's.

What is wanted is an autonomous colleague, on the expectation that agents grow more capable and safer with time: an agent account is given everything a member may be given, by the same rules, and is held back where a member would be, by a role or a membership, never by a second set of rules for its kind. The organization's directory already knows the shape: an account of its own at the identity provider, with someone answerable for it.

## Decision

1. **A member is a person or an agent account, and the rules make no difference between them.** The directory gains `kind`: `person`, as every member has been, or `agent`. An agent account is a row of the same table, and whatever holds for a member holds for it by the same rule: it is let in by an administrator, signs in through the workspace's identity provider with an account of its own there, is listed in projects with a role, owns tasks and projects, is a worker, raises decisions and answers them, writes documents, is in teams, holds a plugin's roles, makes tokens for agents of its own, and may manage people or agents. The pages mark it with a badge, "Agent"; everything that lists people lists it among them.
2. **One thing is kept from it: it is never a workspace administrator.** Configuring the workspace, letting members in and consenting to what a plugin asks stay with people. This is the one rule of the policy that reads `kind`. Whatever else is to be kept from an agent account is kept as it would be from a person: by the project it is not listed in, the role it is not given, the grant a plugin's role was not given to it.
3. **It is who the identity provider says it is, as a person is.** An administrator makes the account, on the Agents page or with `console agent new`: its persona (point 6), the account it will sign in with (the provider and the login, `mira@acme.example` at Google Workspace or `acme-mira` at the git host, derived from the workspace's services, [ADR-0089](0089-a-workspace-names-the-services-its-agents-work-with.md)), its manager and the project it starts in. Making it lets that login in as a listing does (ADR-0022) and writes the member's row at once, so that work can be assigned to it before it has signed in; its first sign-in binds the provider's account to the row. A login a member already carries is made an agent account the same way, once, unless they administer the workspace; `kind` never goes back. The account at the provider is made by a person, since providers ask for one. Until its first sign-in the pages say "invited", which is not a status of the directory: nobody has signed in with it yet, as the People page says of a listed login.
4. **It always has a manager, and a manager is a relation, not an owner.** `managerId` is required of an agent account and names a person or another agent account; a person's stays optional. The line never loops (ADR-0068), so every chain ends at a person. A manager is replaced, never taken away. The account does not belong to its manager and acts for nobody: the manager is whom it turns to first and who is answerable for it. When its manager is out, nothing is reassigned by itself: the administrators supervise it as they always did (point 5), and one of them names the next. A decision is addressed as anyone's is ([ADR-0042](0042-a-decision-is-addressed-to-a-person-its-raiser-unless-another-is-named.md), [ADR-0057](0057-a-decision-may-be-addressed-to-several-people-and-the-first-answer-ends-it.md)), to whomever the asker names, a person or an agent account; the manager is what the base instructions suggest where the agent does not know whom to ask ([ADR-0088](0088-an-agent-account-has-a-desk-and-is-set-up-from-an-invitation-by-signing-in-as-itself.md)), not a rule of the board. `console manager` says whom the asker reports to, for a person as for an agent account, and names the administrators where there is nobody.
5. **Its supervisors are the administrators and the line above it.** Whoever manages it, directly or up the reporting line, and every administrator may watch its sessions, tell it, interrupt it, stop it, start it again with its conversation or with a new one, set the model and the effort it runs with, wake it, see where it is signed in and end a session there, seed and remove its secrets, and keep its persona ([ADR-0085](0085-a-runner-on-the-agents-machine-is-told-by-signals-and-runs-one-session-at-a-time.md) to [ADR-0087](0087-an-agent-account-keeps-its-secrets-in-a-store-of-its-own-that-only-it-reads.md)). Nobody else: an owner of a project it works in sees its runs and reports as anyone's, not its transcript. Supervision is what is added over an agent account, and takes nothing from it. It is one function of the policy, `supervises(asker, agent)`, with its rows in the table test ([ADR-0035](0035-who-may-see-whom-is-decided-by-one-policy-and-the-workspace-keeps-its-settings.md)); and a member sees their own manager and whom they manage, whatever the workspace says of who sees whom.
6. **It has a persona, kept by the console.** A `name` in any language; a `handle`; a `title` (point 7); and an avatar the console draws (point 8). The handle is `[a-z0-9-]`, 2 to 39 characters, beginning and ending with a letter or a digit, one among the workspace's agent accounts, and never a word its addresses use (`new`, `me`, `proposals`, `setup`) or one that follows `console agent` in the command (`invite`, `secret`, `tell`, `stop` and the like); it never changes, as a project's key never does, and is what the pages and the command call the account by: `@mira`, taken wherever the command takes a login. The name and the picture of an agent account are the console's, where a person's are the provider's at each sign-in.
7. **A title is every member's.** `title`, a line of up to 80 characters that says what a member does (`Release engineer`), is kept for a person as for an agent account: by an administrator (`console person title <login> "<title>"`, the People page), and for an agent account by its supervisors too. The pages show it under the name wherever a member is shown with room for a second line: the People page, the member's own page, the pickers, the agent's page; `console people` prints it, and a plugin reads it with the people. It is not `role`, which stays what a member is in the workspace or in a project.
8. **The avatar is a seed and its parts, drawn where it is shown and handed over as a picture file where one is needed.** The account keeps `avatarSeed`, what the proposals rolled, and `avatarParts`, the composition the seed gave or a supervisor changed: flat parts (background, head, ears or none, eyes, mouth, marks, accessory, palette), each an index into the set of shapes the package ships, with that set's version. The shapes are of a few simple kinds, so that one function of the package draws them itself, as SVG and as PNG, with no renderer of anyone else's. Nothing drawn is kept: a person object carries `avatarParts`, some ninety bytes, and the pages draw the picture from it. Where a picture file is needed, for the profile of the agent's account at the messenger or at the identity provider say, the agent gets the file: `console agent <handle> --avatar <file>` writes the PNG, up to 1024 pixels square, and the agent's page offers it to save; how it is put where it goes, through the service's own tool or in a browser, is the agent's. `avatar` stays what it is, the address of a picture at the provider, read through the console ([ADR-0048](0048-a-picture-from-elsewhere-is-read-through-the-console-from-a-public-address-and-kept-a-while.md)), and is `null` for an agent account. A released set of shapes never changes: a new look is a new version, and an avatar keeps the version it was made with. `GET /api/v1/agents/proposals?count=&lang=` answers seeds with their names, handles and parts for whoever makes an account; the names per language and the shapes ship with the package, a deployer replaces them when building a console, and a suggestion never restricts a choice. No picture is uploaded for an agent account in this decision, and no SVG from outside is ever accepted.
9. **It signs in, and what it holds is a session.** In a browser it signs in as anyone does and holds what a person holds there: its pages, its tokens, its sessions. On its machine the command signs in as the account itself (ADR-0088): the connection of ADR-0011, in which the command asks to be the account (`console login --self`) and the account, in its own browser, says "this is me", yields a session of the command's in place of a token, kept for as long as it is used and ended when left unused for `SESSION_TTL_DAYS`, as a browser's session is. An agent at work is never signed out and has nothing to renew, and one that stopped lapses by itself. A session of the command's is presented as a token is and held to what a token may ask (ADR-0021): it makes no token, approves no connection and reaches no session, which stay on the pages for an agent account as for a person. What the account does on a session names nobody beside it, since it did it itself; what an agent of its own does with a token it made names that agent, as ADR-0056 has it. Nothing of this reads `kind`: a person signs a command of their own in the same way, where they work at it themselves. The account sees its sessions and ends any of them, and so do its supervisors, on the console's own pages. Deactivating the account refuses every session and token it holds ([ADR-0044](0044-a-token-of-a-person-who-is-out-is-kept-and-refused-with-the-reason-until-they-are-back-in.md)), which is how an agent account is stopped for good.

## Consequences

- Work is assigned to an agent as to a colleague, asked of it, and read as its own; a person who ran an agent as themselves keeps doing so, since "your agent" changes nothing.
- The policy gains one rule that reads `kind`, never an administrator, and one relation, `supervises`. Nothing else of it changes, and its table test says so: on every other rule, a row with an agent account in a person's place and the same answer.
- The reporting line, kept for the plugins' approvals, becomes the line of supervision as well. A plugin that follows it reaches an agent account who manages people as it reaches a person; whom an organization names manager is its choice.
- A manager with several agents has nothing to renew for them: a session lives while it is used, and the administrators stand in when a manager is out.
- The schema: `kind`, `handle`, `title`, `avatarSeed` and `avatarParts` on `people`; a manager required of an agent account; `hostAccountId` and `lastLoginAt` empty until the first sign-in; a session says whether a browser or the command holds it. The REST API answers `kind`, `handle`, `title` and `avatarParts` on a person, each read as optional, and the agents' routes under `/api/v1/agents`; a change of a title or of a persona is `person.updated` with its `fields`, and a change of an agent's state `agent.state_changed` ([ADR-0085](0085-a-runner-on-the-agents-machine-is-told-by-signals-and-runs-one-session-at-a-time.md)), the two kinds of event this plan adds to the board's feed. The command gains `console agents`, `console agent <handle>`, `console agent new`, `console manager` and `console person title`. The contract is written in [specs/rest.md](../specs/rest.md) and [specs/cli.md](../specs/cli.md) when it is built, and rules 3 and 4 of [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) are worded again in the same change: an agent account is who the provider says it is, and what it keeps of its own, a git-host token among it, is its own.
- Rejected: a separate table for agents, which would duplicate memberships, teams, tokens and attribution and leave every query asking twice; an agent account that never signs in and acts through a token someone made for it, which makes a member a guest of its maker and leaves an expiry to renew for every agent; rules of its own for what it may do, answering only what is addressed to it or asking only its manager, a second permission layer beside the one the board has; an agent account as a workspace administrator, which would let an agent let members in and consent to plugins; several managers or a supervising team, a second structure beside the reporting line; the picture kept as SVG or as an encoded address, a derived copy on every person object where the parts draw it; the picture as SVG alone, which no profile elsewhere takes, or a renderer brought in to draw a few shapes; an address of the console's own for the picture, open to anyone, where the agent only needs the file; an agent account without a manager, which would leave nobody answerable.
