# ADR-0086: A session leaves a transcript that is kept for good

- Status: Accepted; [ADR-0098](0098-a-message-is-on-the-record-where-the-agent-took-it-and-an-interrupt-may-carry-no-words.md) says when what the agent was sent is written: when the product takes it
- Date: 2026-11-10
- Follows [ADR-0085](0085-a-runner-on-the-agents-machine-is-told-by-signals-and-runs-one-session-at-a-time.md). Closes open question 4 of [architecture.md](../architecture.md), what of a run is kept: for a person's agent, its reports, as before; for an agent account, the whole transcript of every session.

## Context

A person's agent is watched by its person, at their own screen; what the board keeps of it is what it reports. An agent account has nobody at its screen. Its supervisors must be able to see what it is doing now, and whoever audits the organization's work later must be able to read what it did, what it was told, by whom, and what it thought while deciding, long after the machine is gone. The transcript is audit data.

Audit data grows without end, and what becomes of the old part is not the console's to decide: whoever keeps the organization's database moves what is past to other storage, seals it away, or keeps it where it is, under procedures of their own and with the database's own tools. The console's part is to lay the data out so that this is easy, and to keep working when a part is away.

## Decision

1. **Every session has a transcript, and the console deletes none of it.** The runner reports every event of the driver (ADR-0085, point 2), in order: what the agent was sent and by whom, what it said, what it thought where the stream carries thinking, every tool it called and what came back, every permission asked and how it was answered, every status and error. An event is a whole block, never a fragment of a stream. The runner numbers the events of a session and sends them in batches, about once a second; the same number sent again changes nothing, so that a batch may be sent twice. Append-only: no route edits or deletes an event, and no retention period of the console's deletes a session.
2. **Bounded at the edge, with the large parts in the blob store.** An event's text is kept in the row up to a bound; a longer payload and every binary go to the blob store, the binaries under the kinds allowed for pictures and the bound on a file's size, up to a bounded number of attachments per event, at a bounded rate per session. What arrives is parsed by schema and shown as text, never run and never rendered as HTML ([AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), rule 2).
3. **Laid out for whoever keeps the database.** The transcript's tables are in a schema of their own, apart from the board's: the sessions, a row each, which stays and says what the session held; and the events, partitioned by the month their session began, so that a session is whole in one partition. Rows hold ids and no foreign keys, so that a month is a table of its own that can be detached, dumped, moved and attached again. The bytes of a month's attachments are kept under that month, apart from the board's files: a table of the month in the store the database keeps, a prefix of the month in a bucket. The worker makes this month's partition and the next one's ahead of time. What becomes of a month that is past is its operator's to do, with the database's and the bucket's own tools and under the organization's own procedure; [deploy/README.md](../../deploy/README.md) says how, and that a month is ready when no session that began in it is open. A session whose month is away is still listed, and says that its events are put away; with the month attached again it reads as before.
4. **Pictures travel both ways.** What the agent's tools returned as a picture and what a supervisor sends with a `tell` are attachments: a `tell` takes files as a comment takes them, and the runner hands them to the driver as image blocks, so that a screenshot pasted on the Live page reaches the agent as a person's would.
5. **Secrets are taken out before an event leaves the machine, and an event can be sealed after.** The runner reads every value of the account's store ([ADR-0087](0087-an-agent-account-keeps-its-secrets-in-a-store-of-its-own-that-only-it-reads.md)) when a session starts and when one changes, and removes them from every whole event before upload, as they are and in the encodings a value is commonly carried in ([ADR-0079](0079-a-secret-is-taken-out-of-an-answer-in-the-encodings-a-service-sends-it-back-in.md)): a secret the agent read or typed into a tool is covered as one the runner handed it. An administrator may seal an event, a secret that slipped through or something that must not be kept: its payload is replaced by a tombstone that says who sealed it, when and why, and the sealing is on the account's record; nothing else of history changes.
6. **Its supervisors read it, and the account itself.** The transcript of an agent account is read by its supervisors (ADR-0084, point 5) and by the account, live on the agent's page (`/agents/<handle>`: the Live view, then its Sessions, its Work and its Settings) and by the command, through `GET /api/v1/agents/<handle>/sessions` and `.../sessions/<id>/events?after=`, a page at a time and as a stream. Whoever watches is woken on a channel of the account's own, not on the board's, which wakes every reader of the feed. Transcript events are not in the board's feed, as a plugin's own events are not, and neither are the session's beginning and end.
7. **It is the console's record of what the runner reported, unchanged since it arrived.** The runner runs on the machine and in the account it records, so the transcript is not proof against an agent that set out to deceive its recorder. What the agent did on the board, at the git host and in the messenger is on those systems' own records, under its own name.

## Consequences

- A supervisor watches an agent as it works, from any browser, and an auditor reads any session as it was, with its thinking where the product exposed it.
- Storage grows with the work; the bounds keep a row small and the blob store takes the rest. A deployment with agent accounts is better served by a bucket than by the store the database keeps: pictures kept for good do not belong in rows.
- Whoever keeps the database works on whole months: a partition and a prefix, with nothing of the board's tied to them. The console needs no command for it and loses nothing when a month is away.
- The one way to remove something from history is to seal the event, on the record. A per-session hash chain is a later addition the shape allows, and what would let a month that was put away be checked.
- A supervisor reads in a transcript what the agent read, a private project's pages among it, whether or not the supervisor is listed there. Listing an agent account in a private project is its owner's decision, taken knowing who supervises it.
- How much thinking a product's streaming protocol carries is the product's; the driver records what it is given, and the Live page says when a session carries none.
- Rejected: reports only, which is what a person's agent keeps and leaves an agent account's work unaccountable; a retention period of the console's own, which would delete audit data on a schedule nobody chose; editing or deleting an event, which an audit trail cannot allow; one table for every month, or the attachments among the board's files, either of which leaves no part that can be moved alone; the events on the board's own channel, which would wake every reader of every feed for each batch; reading the product's own log files from disk, which binds the runner to a format and a path of the product's.
