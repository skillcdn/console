# ADR-0170: A runner says how its agent stands to whoever waits for it, and a wake source shows it

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-28
- Adds to [ADR-0085](0085-a-runner-on-the-agents-machine-is-told-by-signals-and-runs-one-session-at-a-time.md), point 2, what a wake source is; builds on [ADR-0150](0150-a-runner-waits-out-its-products-usage-limit-and-tells-its-agent-before-it.md), [ADR-0162](0162-a-runner-holds-what-waits-for-the-turn-for-the-sub-agents-at-work-in-the-background-too.md) and [ADR-0166](0166-a-wake-carries-what-the-agents-conversation-does-not-hold-and-says-it-once.md), point 1

## Context

Someone who writes to an agent account at a messenger sees nothing until its answer. A turn of a few seconds reads as a colleague who is typing; one of ten minutes, with sub-agents at work, reads as one who is away, and the person writes again or gives up. An agent its product's usage limit stopped answers nothing for hours, and nobody at the messenger knows why. The account's page in the console says all of it, to the few who look there.

A messenger has a place for this: a member's status. An agent account that set its own would spend turns on it and leave it standing whenever a turn ended otherwise than it planned; its runner knows every one of these moments without a turn.

A trial at a workspace, with an agent account's own app, found what the messenger asks: the user scope `users.profile:write`, which an app is installed again to hold, its member's token staying the one it was; and that the fourth call in a row to `users.profile.set` is refused (`429`).

## Decision

1. **The runner says how the agent stands, and a wake source shows it where it can.** `WakeSource.show(host, status, options)`, left out by a source that shows nothing. `status` is one of four: `idle`, nothing to show; `thinking`; `working`; and `limited`, with `until` where the product named the moment its limit resets. The runner decides which, for each source it keeps listening, and the source decides how. `slack` is the one source that has it.
2. **Which is said when.**
   - `limited` first, whatever else goes on: from when the usage limit stops the agent (ADR-0150) until the moment the product named, or until the agent has answered again where it named none. Past the moment the runner brings the agent back in a turn as any other.
   - `working` once the agent has been at work without a pause for longer than a threshold, thirty seconds unless `runner.json` says otherwise, whatever woke it: a turn under way, sub-agents at work in the session's background after it (ADR-0162), and the turn the product starts itself to hand in what they did. A message that arrives meanwhile leaves it.
   - `thinking` for a source from the moment it raises a wake, while that wake is on its way to the agent and while the turn it was handed over in is under way, until the turn ends or the agent is `working`. A wake of the board's, a tell, a briefing, the account's own wake and what another source raised show nothing by themselves. What waits is nothing the agent thinks over while nothing is handed over: under a supervisor's stop, or where the agent could not go on.
   - `idle` otherwise: after a turn, and where the session ended, was stopped or failed for any other reason.
3. **Said when it changes, again while it stands, and once more at the end.** The runner looks after everything it does, and once a second at the least; a source is said again every heartbeat, so that it can keep what it shows from lapsing and try again what failed. As the runner ends, for whichever reason, the last thing it says is `idle` with `last`: what was shown is taken back then, waited for three seconds and no longer.
4. **The `slack` source shows it as the member's own status**, with the member's own token (`slack.user_token`): `users.profile.set` with `status_emoji`, `status_text` and `status_expiration`. `:thought_balloon:` Thinking; `:hammer_and_wrench:` Working · a reply may take a while; `:low_battery:` Out of usage · back at 05:40. The words are in the workspace's language where the package speaks it, from the language packs, English otherwise; the moment is the hour and the minute in the workspace's zone, with its day where it is twenty hours away or more.
5. **`runner.json` says otherwise under `status`**: `after`, the seconds a turn is given, 5 to 600; for `thinking`, `working` and `limited` an `emoji`, by its name between colons, and a `text`, one line of plain words within what a status holds; for `limited` a `back` with `{time}` in it; and `false` for no status at all. It is read by schema as the rest of the file is, a key the runner does not know refused, and what is wrong is said by the key it stands under.
6. **Written sparingly, and every status ends by itself.** Nothing is sent while what stands is what is to be shown. The source allows itself three calls at once and one more every eight seconds, within the ten a minute the messenger documents for one member's profile, and sends the latest when its turn comes, never each change. On a `429` it waits as `Retry-After` says and sends what is to be shown then. `thinking` ends after two minutes, `working` after a quarter of an hour, `limited` at the moment the agent is back, or after half an hour where none is known; a status with less than a minute left is written again when the runner next says it, which is one call in a quarter of an hour for a long turn.
7. **It never costs a wake or a turn.** `show` is waited for by nothing. A refusal, a missing scope, a token of the wrong kind, a workspace that forbids it, is said once in the runner's lines by the messenger's own word, and not tried again until the runner starts again or the account keeps another token; the agent is told once, in a line of the source's own at the end of the next wake that has room for it, as a read that failed is said. A call that failed in passing, the network or an answer that does not read, is said once and tried again when the runner next says how the agent stands. Nothing of the token is in a line, a wake or a file.
8. **The status is the runner's own to take back, and only its own.** The source holds what it set and until when; with nothing to show it takes that back, and where it set none, or its own has ended, it sends nothing and leaves whatever stands. No profile is read to find out. Until when its status stands is kept with what the source keeps from one runner to the next (ADR-0166, point 1): a runner started after one that ended with its status standing takes it back with the first thing it is said.
9. **The app's manifest asks for the scope**, in the skill `running-an-agent`, which says what it is for and that an app made before is installed again to hold it.

## Consequences

- **An app made before shows nothing until it is installed again with the scope.** Its agent is told once, by a line in a wake, each time its runner starts; `"status": false` ends that for an agent that is to show none.
- **While the runner shows a status it is the runner's**: it writes over one the agent, or a person signed in as it, set there, and where none of its own stands it leaves theirs alone. The source cannot give back what it wrote over.
- **`thinking` begins when the source raises the wake**, after it read the conversation the wake carries, which takes five seconds at the most.
- **A call that failed in passing is tried again**, where a refusal is not: a status the source could not take back would otherwise stand until it ends by itself, and one lost connection would end the status for as long as the runner runs.
- **A runner that is gone with its machine leaves a status for a quarter of an hour at the most**, or until the limit it names resets.
- **Not seen in a workspace.** This is written from the messenger's documentation of `users.profile.set` and from the trial's findings, and held by tests with a clock and invented answers. What the messenger answers a workspace that forbids a member's status, and how often it truly takes a call, are seen there.
- **For whoever builds on the package:** `WakeSource.show` with `AgentStatus` and `StatusWords`; `WakeHost.fetch` takes a `body` and answers `status` and `headers` where the host has them; `RunnerOptions.language`; `runner.json` has `status`. A source of one's own that leaves `show` out is said nothing.
- **Rejected:**
  - *The agent sets its own status.* It costs a turn each time, and stands for good after a turn that ended in a failure or a limit, which are the moments it matters.
  - *A message in the conversation, "working on it".* It is one more message for everyone to read, in every conversation the agent is in, and says nothing of when it is over.
  - *Reading the profile, to give back what stood there.* One scope more, a read before every write, and a status put back that its member may have changed meanwhile.
  - *A status with no end, taken back by the runner alone.* A runner that is killed, or whose machine sleeps, would leave an agent at work for days.
  - *Showing it on the account's page.* The page says how the agent stands already; whoever waits at the messenger does not look there.
