# ADR-0164: A plugin says which account at a messenger is a member's, and a wake says by it whether a supervisor wrote

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Settles what [ADR-0160](0160-an-agent-account-takes-every-supervisors-word-as-its-managers-by-a-tell-or-at-the-messenger.md) and [ADR-0163](0163-a-wake-from-the-messenger-is-carried-in-parts-with-what-was-said-before-it-and-an-agent-account-judges-whether-to-answer-where-several-were-woken.md) left open, "who a member of the messenger is in the console", and adds to [ADR-0050](0050-plugin-code-runs-in-a-host-process-of-its-own-and-reaches-the-console-only-through-the-capabilities-it-declared.md)'s catalogue of capabilities. Rests on ADR-0160, points 2 and 8, on ADR-0163, points 1 to 3, and on [ADR-0084](0084-an-agent-account-is-a-member-of-its-own-with-a-manager-above-it.md), point 5.

## Context

An agent account takes a supervisor's own word at the messenger as an instruction (ADR-0160). A wake from the messenger named the account that wrote by the messenger's id for it and said nothing of whose it is: the console kept no member's account at a messenger. So the agent was left to know it for itself, by the address the messenger keeps for the account or by a supervisor's tell that names it, and every agent did that work again, each as well as it could.

Who is matched to which account is known in one place. A plugin that carries the console's notifications to a messenger matches each member to their account there by the work email, which neither side lets its holder set freely, and keeps the match in its own records. Nothing passed it on.

Whoever can say that an account is a member's can make an agent account believe that a supervisor wrote. That is what this decision weighs.

## Decision

1. **A plugin says which account is a member's, under a capability of its own.** `people:accounts` opens `ctx.console.accounts`: `put({ personId, messenger, workspace, account })`, `remove({ personId, messenger, workspace })` and `list({ messenger, workspace })`. The messenger is named as a wake source is (`slack`); the workspace there and the account by the messenger's ids for them, held to the shape of an id. No other capability opens it, `people:read:all` least of all, and it opens nothing else: an administrator consents to it by name, reading that an agent account takes a supervisor's word by it.
2. **The console keeps what was said, with who said it.** `messenger_accounts`: one member for an account, and one account of a member in a workspace of a messenger. What a plugin said before of the account or of the member there gives way to what it says now. What another plugin said of either stands while it counts (point 3), and the call is refused (`plugin.conflict`): no plugin says over another. What counts no more gives way, so that the plugin in the place of one that was disabled is not kept out by it. The member is active when it is said. A workspace keeps so many, of every messenger together.
3. **What was said counts only while the plugin that said it is enabled and holds the capability.** Read at the moment the console is asked: a plugin disabled, waiting for consent or with the capability taken back has said nothing, and its rows stand unread until it holds the capability again. A member who is deactivated is nobody. A plugin that is purged takes what it said with it, each as an event: a package installed later under its key has said nothing until it says it.
4. **Every change is an event of the workspace under the plugin's name.** `person.updated` with the field `account`: the member's login, the messenger, the workspace and the account, and `set`, whether the account is theirs now or no longer, in the transaction that makes the change. A change a person's own call made names the person, and their agent, as its actor (ADR-0056); one of a job, a hook or the plugin as itself names the plugin alone. It is said as a change of the member, and not as a kind of event of its own, so that a command of before reads the feed it is on ([ADR-0043](0043-the-rest-contract-is-held-to-a-snapshot-taken-at-the-floors.md)). The workspace's feed keeps them, so whoever reads it sees later when an account became a member's and by which plugin.
5. **An agent account asks the console who wrote, for itself.** `POST /api/v1/agents/me/writers`, on a session of the account's own: accounts at a messenger, a hundred at most, each answered with `told`, whether the console is told the accounts of that workspace at all, and `member`, the active member the account is, by login, with their kind and whether they supervise the account that asks. A supervisor is who ADR-0084 says: an administrator, or whoever is above the account in the reporting line. It is asked of nobody's supervisors but the asker's own, and by no person. A member the asker may not see, where the workspace narrows who sees whom ([ADR-0035](0035-who-may-see-whom-is-decided-by-one-policy-and-the-workspace-keeps-its-settings.md)), is said to be a member, with no login, unless they are in its reporting line: whom its work answers to it is told of whatever the projects say.

   **The relation is read whole, and both ways.** A supervisor is the account's manager, everyone above the manager to the top of the line, and every administrator, in the line or out of it; `supervisor` is all of them, and never the manager alone. Beside it the answer says how: `administrator`, and `line`, where the member stands in the asker's reporting line, `manager` whom it reports to, `above` anyone further up, `report` whoever reports to it, `below` anyone further down at any depth, `null` nowhere in it. The line never loops, so a member is above or below and never both; an administrator may stand anywhere in it, below the asker too, and supervises it all the same. Each of these is a case of its own in the tests: the manager, the manager's manager, an administrator out of the line, one above in it and one below, a report, a report's report, and a member beside the account under one manager, who is nothing to it.
6. **A wake says it in the source's own line.** The `slack` source asks before it writes a wake, and after the account's id the line says `(@kim in the console, a supervisor of yours: your manager)`, `(@nova in the console, an agent account, no supervisor of yours)`, or `(no member the console knows)` for an account the console is told nothing of in a workspace it is told the accounts of, which is all it says: a plugin that is still matching, or that could match a supervisor to no account, has said less than there is, and a supervisor's tell that names the account tells what it did not. The line of each earlier message a wake carries says the same, and so does each line of `console agent messages`; and the wake's last line says it of each other agent account it names (ADR-0163, point 9), since how one of them stands to the agent is among what it judges by whether to answer. After `a supervisor of yours` the line says how, in a few words: `your manager`, `above your manager`, `an administrator`, or two of them (`your manager and an administrator`); and of a member below the agent in its line, that they report to it: `no supervisor of yours, reports to you`, `reports to you through others` at any depth below, and `a supervisor of yours: an administrator, who reports to you` for an administrator who does. An agent takes every supervisor's word alike (ADR-0160): how one is a supervisor is what it judges by whom to turn to and who is better placed, and makes nobody one whom `supervisor` does not.
7. **No message can write it.** It stands in a line of the source's own, which the agent reads under its session's mark and which no word of a message reaches (ADR-0163, points 2 and 3). What it says is the console's answer to the account's own sign-in for that account in that workspace: the source asks by the id and the workspace the messenger's event names for the writer, never by the agent's own workspace where the event names none, hears only an answer for what it asked, and writes a login into the line only where it is shaped as one. A name the messenger shows, a title, a note and what a message says of its writer are not read for it.
8. **Where either side says nothing, a wake is as it was.** A console that does not know the question, one that fails or does not answer within the one wait a wake has for all it reads (ADR-0163, point 5), a workspace no plugin told it the accounts of, a client or a source that asks nothing, and a wake that is raised with nothing read, where too many wait or the source ends: the line names the account as the messenger does, and the agent knows a supervisor's account as ADR-0160, point 2, has it. A plugin that asks for the capability as one it can go without runs as before until an administrator turns it on.
9. **The agent goes by the line where it says, and is told so, in fewer words than before.** A session's first words say who the supervisors are, that the line above a message says whose the account is where the console knows it, and that a supervisor's tell that names the account, or the address the messenger keeps for it, tells it where the console does not know. The desk's template says none of that again: a session reads both, and the first words are what no edit of the desk takes away. What the first words said of a message passed on is said in the wake's own line (ADR-0163, point 4), and what they said of writing on the board what a word at the messenger began is the desk's and the skill's. So of ADR-0160, point 9, the desk says less under "Where I work" and "Safety", and the third line of the first words less at its end; what they mean is unchanged.
10. **A message written through an app in a member's name is that member's word.** The line says both, `through an app` and who the account is: an agent account writes so, and so does a person whose own agent writes for them. ADR-0160, point 2, goes by the account and not by how it wrote, as the console goes by whose sign-in a command holds; what an app of a supervisor's echoes of somebody else's words is the supervisor's to keep out of their account.

## Consequences

- **A plugin that holds `people:accounts` can make an agent account take anyone's account at that messenger for a supervisor's.** That is the capability's reach, written in the catalogue and in the sentence an administrator consents to. What bounds it: the install path, the consent by name, the event for every change under the plugin's name, and the alarm of ADR-0160, point 1, which holds whoever asks. A match is as sure as what the plugin matched by: the messenger plugin matches by the work email, which the identity provider or an administrator wrote on the console's side and the workspace's administrators govern on the messenger's.
- **What a supervisor's account at the messenger carries is unchanged.** Whoever holds it instructs the accounts that supervisor supervises, as ADR-0160 says; the line makes the agent sure of whose account it is, not of who holds it.
- **The change is a people event.** A plugin that reads the directory (`people:read:all`) hears them with their content, as it hears who came and went: which member has which account at a messenger, for the plugins an administrator let read everyone.
- **A wake asks the console once, and once more where others wrote what it carries.** First who wrote the message that woke, beside the read of the conversation and not after it, so that a messenger that does not answer costs the wake's first line nothing; then, at once, whoever else wrote the earlier messages it carries or is named in its last line. The account's own session asks, and both questions are within the five seconds the wake has for all it reads (ADR-0163, point 5): a console that is slow costs the wake what it would say and no more of the wait than is left.
- **An agent account's own account is said as one.** `an agent account` after a login is the console's word for what `through an app` only suggests. Where several agents were woken, each still judges for itself whether to answer (ADR-0163, point 8), and now reads of each of the others whether it is a supervisor of its own.
- **For whoever builds on the package:** `WakeHost.writers` is there for a wake source to ask by, where the client has `writers`; `ctx.console.accounts` is left out by a console older than the capability, and a plugin that runs on both asks whether it is there.
- **Rejected:**
  - the console matching members to accounts itself, with a token of the messenger's kept on the server: the console holds no credential of a messenger's, and one plugin already does the matching under an administrator's consent;
  - members saying their own account: a member who names a supervisor's account as their own is then no supervisor, but one who names their own as a supervisor's would be, unless every claim were proved at the messenger, which is the plugin's work;
  - the capability folded into `people:read:email` or `people:read:all`: a plugin that reads addresses would then instruct agents, with nobody having consented to that;
  - the answer written into the wake by the console when the signal is raised: the runner writes a wake's text and reads it back by its marks (ADR-0163), and a console that rewrote it would be a second writer of what only the runner reads;
  - one question for the whole wake, after the conversation is read: a messenger that does not answer would take the wait with it, and the line that says whether a supervisor wrote is what the agent needs most;
  - the member's name in the line, which is the member's own to write and would put words of theirs under the session's mark;
  - the agent's own workspace taken for the writer's where the event names none: the same id at another organization is another account, and the line would say of it what the console said of a colleague.
- **Not decided here:** how many levels lie between, which the line does not say past the first; a page of the console that lists a member's accounts at a messenger, the events being what there is to read; and a messenger other than Slack, whose plugin and wake source would say and ask in the same way.
