# ADR-0035: Who may see whom is decided by one policy, and the workspace keeps its settings

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Builds on [ADR-0005](0005-people-sign-in-through-an-identity-provider-and-membership-and-roles-are-the-consoles-own.md), [ADR-0008](0008-a-workspace-holds-projects-and-what-a-person-may-see-and-change-is-decided-per-project.md), [ADR-0021](0021-a-token-configures-as-its-person-does.md) and [ADR-0022](0022-who-is-in-is-the-consoles-own-record-and-an-administrator-keeps-it.md). The contract is in [specs/rest.md](../specs/rest.md) and [specs/cli.md](../specs/cli.md); the rules are `apps/console/src/auth/policy.ts`.

## Context

The People page showed everyone of the workspace to every member. A console that runs the organization's own work beside outside participants and customers, each in a private project, should show a person the people they work with and no one else. The Members page of a project open to the workspace offered to add and remove people as a private project does, though everyone of the workspace is a member of it already, which misled. And the decisions about who sees whom and what were spread over the queries and the routes: a condition on project visibility in one module, a check of the administrator's role inline in another, each right, none in one place; finer control is coming, and every rule added that way is a rule that can be forgotten in one route and bypassed through another.

## Decision

1. **One policy decides.** `apps/console/src/auth/policy.ts` holds every rule about who may see whom and what: the one condition of membership in a project (a project open to the workspace has everyone of it, a private one those listed), which project a person sees, who may work in a project, which people a person sees and may find, who reads the logins let in and the workspace's own events, and who configures the workspace. A query takes its condition from the policy and applies it in SQL, so that a listing, a lookup and a reference agree and nothing is filtered after the fact; a route names its guard, who is asking and with what, and asks nothing of its own. What cannot be decided is denied. A new rule is a function in the policy, with its row in the table test beside it, never a check in a route.
2. **The workspace keeps the settings a rule depends on.** They are columns of the workspace's row, kept by an administrator on the Settings page, or through their agent's token (ADR-0021), with `PATCH /api/v1/workspace`, and read on every request that depends on them, so that a change holds from the next request on and nothing is kept in memory; `GET /api/v1/me` says them, and the board hears `workspace.updated` with the fields that changed. They are the organization's choices, not the operator's: configuration stays with what the deployment is made of.
3. **Who sees whom is the first setting.** `peopleVisibility` is `everyone`, which is what the console did, or `shared_projects`: a member sees the active people who are in a project they are in, themselves, and the administrators, whom anyone may need to turn to; an administrator sees everyone whatever the setting. The logins let in and the organization's reading, and the workspace's own events, say who is in beyond the projects one shares, so they go only to whom the policy shows everyone. A project open to the workspace has everyone in it: a member of one sees everyone, which is the condition and not an exception, and a console that keeps its people apart keeps its projects private.
4. **An owner finds anyone by login.** An owner must be able to list a person the page does not show them, so `GET /api/v1/people?login=` finds one person by their exact login, for an owner of a project anyone active of the workspace, for everyone else whom they see. An owner thereby learns that a login is one of the workspace, and nothing more; the command's `member`, `person` and `--assignee` take a login that way.
5. **A project open to the workspace lists its owners and shows everyone.** Its members are everyone active of the workspace, each with their role, an owner where a listing or the role of administrator says so, a member otherwise, and whether a listing holds them; nobody is added to it or removed from it, since everyone is in it; an owner is made from a row, and an owner made a member again loses the listing and stays the member everyone is. A private project keeps its list as it was.

## Consequences

- The migration adds `people_visibility` to the workspace, `everyone` by default: nothing changes for a console that says nothing.
- `GET .../members` answers everyone for a project open to the workspace, with `listed` on every member, and `POST` with a role of `member` is refused there as `member.open_project`; `GET /api/v1/people` may narrow, and takes `login`; a console built on the package reads the new fields as optional. Said as `Breaking:` in the entry, since what exists changed.
- The next rule, a read-only role, a person's own say over who sees their work, a customer's project that hides its people from each other, is a function in the policy and a setting on the workspace, and the tests of the policy say what each decides for each role.
- Rejected: the setting in the environment (the organization's choice would need a redeploy, and the operator is not the organization); filtering in the routes (the same rule written twice, once for the list and once for the lookup, and forgotten the third time); hiding the administrators too (someone must be there to turn to); a lookup by partial login (a way to read the whole list one letter at a time); keeping the Members page of an open project as it was (it said something false).
