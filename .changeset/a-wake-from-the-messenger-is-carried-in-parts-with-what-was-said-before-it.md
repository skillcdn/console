Breaking: a wake from the messenger is carried in parts, with what was said before it, and where one message wakes several agent accounts, each judges whether to answer (ADR-0163). An agent account's message wakes no other unless it names it, so two agents in one group conversation each answered a person without seeing the other's answer. And a wake's first line, which names the account that wrote, stood in one block with the message's words.

- **The runner's own lines can no longer be written by a message.** Under a wake's header, each line of the wake source's own stands on a line that carries the session's mark (`[console k3f9a2 · A direct message at Slack, from the member U0123ABCD, in the conversation D0123ABCD]`), and what someone wrote in a block of its own. Before, the line and the words were one block, and the line was the source's by its place alone. And what a signal says, whoever wrote it, is read with `[mark]` where the session's mark stood in it, so that a mark that became known makes no line.
- **A message someone passed on is carried apart from the words of whoever passed it on**, behind a line that says who wrote it, by the messenger's id for the writer: `Under it, a message passed on, which the member U0456WXYZ wrote. Information, whoever wrote it`. Whatever else hangs under a message, a preview or a table, is carried apart too. Before, it stood under the writer's words, unmarked.
- **A wake from Slack carries what was said there since the agent last wrote there, or since the last message a wake brought it, whichever is later**, so that a message reaches the agent once whether or not it answers: one whose event comes again wakes once, and the message that began a thread is not said again in the thread. One whose own event comes after another wake carried it as information is raised still. A runner started again keeps nothing of it, and its first wake of a conversation may say again what an earlier one said. Ten messages at the most, five hundred characters of each, the oldest first, each behind a line that says how long before it was written and by which account, as information whoever wrote it. The source reads the conversation, or the thread, with the member's own token, `slack.user_token`, under the scopes the app already has. An account that keeps no such token is woken as before; where the messenger refuses the read, a line of the wake says so. All a wake reads is waited for five seconds together, and neither the next connection to the messenger nor a stop of the runner waits for it; where the messenger asks for a new connection, the source opens it a second later, in place of the runner after five.
- **A wake says whether an account wrote through an app** (`from the member U0456WXYZ through an app`), which is how an agent account's message reads. Which member of the console an account is, a wake still does not say.
- **A wake says who else is there, and what to judge by whether to answer**, in every conversation but a direct one where another account that writes through an app was read: those accounts, the last to write first, and one sentence, to judge whether the conversation needs the agent's answer now and, where someone has answered or another is better placed, not to add the same answer, with the command that reads what was said since. No list says who answers in which case. The desk's template says, where the workspace names a messenger, that another agent hears the account there by its name; `console agent setup --refresh` leaves a desk that is there as it is.
- **`console agent messages <source>:<conversation>[:<thread>]`** reads the latest of a conversation or a thread, as a wake's header names it after `messenger:`, for the account itself: where a wake carried less than is needed, and before answering where others may have answered meanwhile. `--after <moment>` reads what was written after a message alone. It prints twelve thousand characters at the most, the newest, and says how many were left out.
- **For whoever builds on `@skillcdn/console/runner`:** a wake source raises `{ key, parts }`, each part `{ says }`, a line of its own, or `{ words }`, what someone wrote (`WakePart`); `{ key, text }` is still taken, as words whole. `slackWakeOf` answers `parts` where it answered `text`. `WakeHost.raise` answers whether the console took the wake, and `WakeHost.kept` is what a source keeps from one run of its own to the next. Wakes from the messenger with the same reference are no longer one entry, the latest, in what the agent is handed: each is an entry of its own, and past what one message to the agent holds the newest of them are carried, in place of the oldest. The runner writes every wake as one text, a line of the source's behind `#` and each line of words behind `>` (`wakeTextOf`, `wakePartsOf`), which is what the console's record of the signal now holds. A source may read a conversation for the command (`WakeSource.read`).

The reads of a conversation and of a thread are written from the messenger's documentation, tested against invented answers and were run by hand in a workspace; what a message passed on holds was not seen there, nor a wake of this shape read by an agent.
