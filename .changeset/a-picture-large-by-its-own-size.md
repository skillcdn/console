Breaking: the picture overlay measures by the picture's own size. A picture longer than the window was cut at the window's edge, top and bottom, and could be neither moved nor made smaller, so that the rest of it was never seen. Now a picture opens with all of it in view; one much longer than the window opens as wide as the window has room for, from its top, and one much wider as tall as there is room for, from its left, so that what is shown can be read. It zooms out until all of it is in view and in to eight times its own size, the figure in the bar being the scale against its own pixels (100% is a pixel for a pixel) and the way back to all of it; a double press looks at its own size where it was pressed, and from there shows all of it again; a drag, the arrow keys, Page Up, Page Down, Home and End move it as far as there is more of it. A press on the picture no longer closes the overlay, the keys are heard after a drag as before it, and a long file name no longer widens the overlay past a phone's screen. For whoever builds a console: `zoomAround` and `panBy` take the picture's size and the stage's; `wholeScale`, `openingView`, `boundView`, `LONG_PICTURE` and the type `PictureSize` are new; `clampScale`, `FITTED` and `MIN_SCALE` are gone, since a scale of 1 is the picture's own size and no longer the fitted one. `Lightbox`, `PictureThumb`, `usePicture` and `PictureOverlay` are used as before.
