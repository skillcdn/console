A plugin's settings page reads at a glance, with as few words as it takes. Its name, state, key and version are in the head, with the way to disable it; what it is on and holds is a row each (its board project, its projects, the capabilities it holds, its commands and queries, what agents read of it), where the facts stood in a grid that broke. Enabling is one form in three parts: the capabilities, each by a name in the person's language with one sentence of what it opens (`plugins.capability` in the packs), ticked one by one, those consented to before ticked already; the board project; the projects. A secret shows when it was set, with its field and buttons on one line. The roles are the plugin's own, each with what it is for and who holds it, every member for the default role (`defaultRole`, which `GET /api/v1/plugins/<key>` now names), each grant a chip with the way to revoke it, and one choice to grant it to a person, the members of a project or everyone. Export and purge are two rows of one panel, the purge's field offered once the plugin is disabled. The paragraphs that explained the capabilities, the roles and the purge are gone; `PluginSettingsView` takes the workspace's `people`. A `person` or `project` setting is chosen among the workspace's by name and held as its id, where the form took an id typed by hand. The Korean pack says 활성 and 비활성 of a plugin, and 활성화 and 비활성화 of the ways, as it does of people, where it said 켜짐, 꺼짐, 켜기 and 끄기.
