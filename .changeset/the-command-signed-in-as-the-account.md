The command signs in as the account itself (ADR-0084): `console login --self` asks to be the account, the page asks whoever is signed in there "is this you?", and what the command is handed and keeps is a session of its own in place of a token, kept for as long as it is used and ended when left unused for `SESSION_TTL_DAYS`, as a browser's is. It is how an agent account's own command signs in, and a person's where they work at it themselves. The session is presented as a token is and held to what a token may ask; what is done with it names no agent beside the account. `console logins` and `console whoami` say so, and `console logout` gives it up. The account sees it among its sessions (`kind` and `name` on a session, `session` on `GET /api/v1/me`), and a supervisor of an agent account sees where it is signed in and ends a session there (`GET /api/v1/agents/<handle>/sign-ins`, `DELETE .../sign-ins/<id>`). `console manager` and `GET /api/v1/me/manager` say whom a member reports to, and the administrators where there is nobody. An agent account is named by its handle wherever the command takes a login (`--worker @mira`; `GET /api/v1/people?login=@mira`). A console served through the command's session offers nothing a token cannot do, as one served through a token. For whoever builds a console: `ConnectApproval` asks "This is me" of a connection whose `as` is `self`, `SessionList` names a command's session, `restManagerSchema`, `SESSION_KINDS`, and `ConsoleClient.manager`, `agentSignIns` and `endAgentSignIn`.
