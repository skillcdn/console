A secret is sealed by the console from an answer the plugin received, and goes where its setting says (ADR-0070). `ctx.fetch` takes `seal: { <setting>: "$.path" | { path, expiresIn } }`, values of the answer's JSON the console seals as the plugin's `secret` settings before the plugin reads the answer, which says `"[sealed]"` in their place, with their expiry; a request carries `secret` or `secrets`, five at most, each `as` `header:<name>`, `bearer`, `query:<name>`, `basic` or `body:<field>`. A `secret` setting may name `hosts`, the hosts it goes to and no other, and `use: "verify"`, one that verifies signatures and signs nothing. `ctx.secrets.has` is false past the expiry, and `ctx.secrets.status(name)` answers `{ set, setAt, expiresAt, source }`, `source` being `administrator` or the host the value was sealed from; `createTestContext` answers it too. A setting on `GET /api/v1/plugins/<key>` carries `expiresAt`, `source`, `hosts` and `use` (read as optional), the plugin's settings page and `console plugin <key> settings` say from where and until when, and the feed says a secret was sealed from a host's answer (`plugin.configured` with `source`).
